Who contributed to the Matthew effect?

The examined sources document who was credited with naming the effect, whose research informed the 1968 paper and where an earlier biblical analogy appeared.

Direct contributions

Robert K. Merton

Robert King Merton · 1910–2003 · Sociologist, Columbia University

A newspaper report dated 19 May 1964 attributes the naming of the effect to Merton. He was the sole listed author of the 1968 paper. His later judgment that the paper warranted joint authorship concerned the extent of his reliance on Zuckerman’s research.

  • 1964 newspaper report, pp. 6–7
  • 1968 article, pp. 56–63
  • Later acknowledgment, p. 607 n. 2
  • Columbia biography
  • Portrait reference

Harriet Zuckerman

Harriet Anne Zuckerman · Born 1937 · Sociologist, Columbia University

Zuckerman’s interviews with Nobel laureates provided part of the empirical basis of the 1968 analysis. In her retrospective she credits Merton with naming and explaining the pattern.

  • 1968 article, p. 56
  • Zuckerman’s retrospective, pp. 130–132
  • American Academy profile
  • Columbia faculty profile
  • Portrait reference

The disputed textbook attribution

Sam Silverman recalled the biblical wording from the Fiesers’ 1944 Organic Chemistry, and N. David Mermin initially considered whether it might establish priority. In April 2005 Mermin reported that p. 119 of the 1950 edition quotes Matthew 25:29 in a chemical context and does not use the term “Matthew effect”.

Merton’s 1968 note independently names the Fiesers’ Introduction to Organic Chemistry (1957). For the 1950 passage, the evidence shown here is Mermin’s published report of the page.

  • Silverman’s recollection
  • Mermin’s January reply
  • Mermin’s April correction

Mary Peters Fieser

Chemist · Co-author of Introduction to Organic Chemistry (1957)

Named with Louis as co-author of the textbook in Merton’s footnote. A separately dated preface credits her with writing numerous chapters and preparing the index of their earlier Organic Chemistry.

  • Merton’s textbook note, 1968, p. 63 n. 50
  • Signed preface, 30 March 1944, p. iv
  • Science History Institute biography
  • Portrait reference

Louis Frederick Fieser

Born 1899 · Chemist · Co-author of Introduction to Organic Chemistry (1957)

Named with Mary as co-author of the 1957 textbook. His signed preface to their earlier Organic Chemistry gives his account of Mary’s contributions to that book.

  • Merton’s textbook note, 1968, p. 63 n. 50
  • Signed preface, 30 March 1944, p. iv
