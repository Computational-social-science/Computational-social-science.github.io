Measuring scientific visibility and testing historical credit

Candidate measurement protocol, version 0.2 · Matthew Effect worked example · 20 November 2026

Did an already visible scientist receive credit for a particular contribution made by somebody else? This question needs two independently constructed records. One identifies the contributor and the role from dated evidence. The other measures how visible each eligible person was before a later author had an opportunity to credit that role. The time ordering also allows a separate question: does receiving credit predict subsequent visibility?

1. Fix the claim and the population

Define historical scientific fame as the extent to which an identifiable scientist was known to a specified audience at a specified time. Unaided recall, recognition on prompting, visibility in published books and expert professional esteem represent different observations. This study directly observes written exposure in specified publishing populations. Its use as a proxy for contemporary recognition requires independent validation. Books differ in readership and print run. One book is one represented work, irrespective of how many people read or remembered it. State the audience and intended use of an estimate before selecting a source.

Register the concept, sense, contribution role, eligible people, language, corpus release, observation period and information cutoff before scoring outcomes. Distinguish naming, formulation, empirical work, authorship and later application. Keep the date of the act, date of its earliest examined witness and date of retrospective testimony separately. A dated use supplies an upper bound on the date of naming. Evidence of an earlier idea can move the history of the concept without moving the first attested use of the term.

For each role, keep a source-located finding, its strongest competing reading, and the observation that would change the finding. Freeze this record before extracting later attributions. A later paper about unequal recognition supplies an opportunity only if its passage addresses the relevant act. An interviewee's experience with scientific reward supplies evidence about that experience. The attribution of the Matthew effect requires evidence about naming, formulating or investigating that particular concept.

2. Reproduce the historical measure before adapting it

Michel et al. examined the frequency of selected written names in digitized books. Their original annual frequency used occurrences divided by words published that year. They ranked candidate tracking names by total mentions from birth through 2000 and checked ambiguity. A candidate passed the birth screen if its mean frequency across birth year ±10 was below 10⁻⁹ or at least tenfold lower than its birth-to-2000 mean. The latter window includes posthumous years for some people. The supplement's 80% comparison applies to a candidate trigram and its terminal bigram. Figure S20 shows why “Oliver Lodge” can be more suitable than “Oliver Joseph Lodge”, and why the short form “Mary Moore” is unsuitable for Mary Tyler Moore. Ranking historical cohorts using mentions through 2000 answers a retrospective question. Predicting a 1964 attribution requires a past-only name selection rule as well as past-only counts. See the main paper, Fig. 3C–F (printed p. 179), and supplement III.7A–B. Michel et al., article and supplement

A calibration reconstructs the original English 1964 frequency of “evolution” from 80,039 occurrences and 2,452,549,636 words, giving 0.0000326350. The current English Viewer returns an annual fraction for the queried string relative to n-grams of its order. Its annual response lacks the individual books, hit counts and word denominators required to reconstruct a person's mentions per word. Keep the corpus edition and denominator attached to every value. 3. Resolve people and register searchable spellings

Two analyses use different information sets. The historical benchmark asks whether the original tracking rule can select the published name using its own observations through 2000. The event-specific analysis freezes the identity evidence and count window at 31 December 1963. A form first documented in October 1964 is admissible for study of the published article in 1968, but needs an earlier witness for the May 1964 analysis. A later authority can identify a historical person retrospectively; using it to choose a predictor is a separate sensitivity analysis. If the birth window is incomplete by the cutoff, the screen is unevaluable. If two candidate spellings have incompatible or unknown denominators, their Viewer fractions cannot choose a winner by total mentions.

Give each person a stable identity. Record birth, death where known, attested bylines and their dates, authority sources, institutions, fields, coauthors, works and plausible collisions. Preserve punctuation, casing, order and the original occurrence. A spelling can belong in a person's alias registry while an occurrence still refers to somebody else. Store a status for each occurrence: accepted for a person, assigned to another person, unresolved, or artefact. Record the evidence used to decide.

4. Publish three series with their own denominators

A birth-year check with an exact-name collision (21 November 2026). Louis Frederick Fieser, the chemist born in 1899, had a father with exactly the same full name. In the July 2024 English Google Books corpus, the unsmoothed, case-sensitive query for that full name returned positive values for 1889 and 1890, two of the ten preceding calendar years examined, and for 86 of the 124 years from 1899 through 2022. These are counts of years with a positive string frequency. To identify the referents in a given year, inspect dated occurrences and their roles. Earlier namesakes and catalog or OCR errors are competing explanations for a pre-birth hit. A cutoff at birth can remove anachronistic years while leaving a mixture of people in later years. For the same coverage and date, ten mentions in one of ten works and one mention in each of ten works have equal mention counts. Their work prevalence is 0.1 and 1.0. Record work, edition, scan and page separately. Audit reprints, duplicate scans, citation copying and translations. Where different editions are the object of study, report edition reach separately. If the frame is pages, count the words on exactly the pages on which mentions were sought.

For sampled works with known inclusion probability π, use weight w = 1/π. Let M be the weighted total mentions, W the weighted words, K the weight of works with a mention and N the eligible work weight, using the same works throughout. Then U = 10⁹ M/W and B = K/N. When K > 0, U/10⁹ = B × (M/K) ÷ (W/N). Hence a change in intensity may reflect wider reach, more repetition within works already mentioning the person, or a change in book length. With K = 0, the conditional average M/K is undefined. Changing the text sections searched changes both numerator and word denominator. Source metadata and research notes about a book stay outside its text.

Report observed lower and upper values by treating unresolved candidate mentions as zero or one for each eligible person in turn. For prevalence, count the union of affected works. These are ambiguity bounds conditional on the extracted candidates. They do not cover missed OCR names, unobserved pages, corpus selection or sampling error. For probability samples, report sample inclusion probabilities, weights and uncertainty clustered by work or source family. For targeted witnesses, report raw counts, denominators and sensitivity to removing each work. A year with no eligible readable material has an undefined estimate.

5. Separate age, period and event time

An arithmetic expression in the Viewer supplies a further empirical test. For the three registered Zuckerman aliases, the linked expression returned precisely the “Harriet Zuckerman” series in all 86 years, both with smoothing 0 and smoothing 3. The initialed spelling returns positive values when queried separately, the expanded name has no returned series, and the expression displays a space before the period. An independently queried “Harriet A . Zuckerman” returned no series. Group membership in the identity registry cannot recover missing counts from an aggregate response. Inspect the requested Viewer expression

Keep unsmoothed, case-sensitive series for estimation. Smoothing 3 uses a centred display window and can introduce post-event information into a plotted pre-event year. Publish equal-year averages and their coverage. Calculate a pooled rate only with compatible annual counts and denominators. Keep prebirth values for error diagnosis. Birth and death years are partial years of life. Posthumous reception is a distinct outcome. For Zuckerman, the last observed corpus year is an observation boundary.

Compare common calendar windows (shared publishing conditions), common ages (different calendar periods) and event-relative windows (complete years before and after a dated act). Annual age indices use publication year minus birth year. For a precisely dated event use completed age on its date. On 19 May 1964 Merton and Zuckerman were 53 and 26. On 5 January 1968 they were 57 and 30. Period, birth cohort and age are linked arithmetically. Two aligned curves cannot identify three independent effects. Show the event year, Merton's death year and the posthumous period separately.

6. Extend the portrait only when the data support it

Keep text exposure, book reach, scholarly reception and persistence as separate columns. A scholarly-reception measure needs resolved author and work identifiers, deduplicated citing works, citing publication dates and a frozen database snapshot. Use annual counts or a declared field/year denominator, and separate self-citation when the question calls for it. For persistence, fix the observable, threshold, window and minimum coverage in advance. Report years exceeding the threshold divided by observed eligible years, together with that fraction's numerator, denominator and missing years. Ending the series in 2022 right-censors persistence. A half-life requires an observed peak and enough subsequent observations under a declared fitting rule.

For an exploratory composite, define a field, period, roles and inclusion mechanism independently of measured visibility. Transform each eligible component to its reference-cohort midrank percentile, z = (midrank − 0.5)/n. A monotonic log transformation leaves these ranks unchanged. With non-negative weights summing to one, F = 100 × Σ wⱼzⱼ. Publish the components, weights, cohort size, ties and rank changes across alternative weights. Withhold the index when a required component or reference distribution is missing. A visibility-ranked historical cohort cannot serve as an independent sample for estimating how common fame is. No calibrated composite or population percentile is available for the Matthew pair.

7. Test competing explanations

The proposed mechanism runs from earlier visibility to later role-specific attribution and from that attribution to subsequent visibility. Age, duration of career, field, institution, publication genre, language and access to earlier accounts can affect both exposure and outcome. Draw the eligible contributor roster from independent dated records. Selecting only people with detectable name series, available passages or high later fame can alter the association.

8. Evidence required before releasing a reusable measure

The cross-disciplinary pilot draws 100 bibliographic records in each of 1959–1963 and 1969–1973. The unit assigned to a year depends on the catalog definition, which the frame receipt must identify. Maintain the original selections as the denominator for access checks. A year without a match in every complete book differs from a year for which the books could not be read. For unknown outcomes, let Y be the weighted sum of confirmed positive works among sampled works, A the weight whose outcome is unknown, and N the sampled work weight. Conditional on correct ascertainment in the known portion, the sampled-frame coverage lies in [Y/N, (Y+A)/N]. These bounds are distinct from sampling intervals and can extend from zero to one. Missing text lengths also prevent an all-frame mention-intensity estimate. Show the available-text denominator separately.

9. Reproduction and revision

For each run retain query text, date, release, parameters, original response and checksum, annual values, aliases, occurrence decisions, denominators, eligible work list, executable transformation and unit checks. Check an original page, replay from retained inputs, distinguish absent series from zero, verify denominator compatibility, and compare results across plausible aliases, time windows and work families. Label archival reproduction, proxy measurement, source interpretation and causal identification independently. Record which observation changed when revising a conclusion. Findings used to develop this protocol are retrospective. Freeze the sampling and coding rules before examining the next independent batch.

10. What the implementation changes

English book feasibility. The two pilot windows each yielded 100 distinct selected works, 20 per year. For each randomly drawn work rank in Open Library we required an edition whose own date and language matched the year and English. The work and its candidate editions were ordered by identifier before taking the matching edition. This matters because Open Library normally boosts readable editions. Annual index counts, boundary checks and retained responses support replay; the live index provided no immutable collection version. Discipline was measured after the year-stratified draw: 94 of 200 records lacked a Library of Congress class. Design weights apply conditionally to this catalog index. Open Library Search API

At the exact-edition Internet Archive check, five items yielded OCR with a matching scan manifest. Of the others, 55 were restricted, 132 had no exact locator at the checked provider and eight had a different or unconfirmed edition. Four scan candidates fell in the early window and one in the later window. Searching 415,174 extracted word tokens yielded zero candidate name spans, including the broader surname search. Independent visual reading of ten pages drawn without using names found zero target occurrences. The precision and recall denominators were both zero. One scanned summer-course compilation combines 1963 and 1969 material. Removing its 140,654 words leaves 274,520 raw OCR words in four date-aligned candidates, with digitizer inserts, volume boundaries and image-only material still to assess. An alternate full-view record for a 1962 draw turned out to be a 1950 edition. This pilot measures what can be recovered. The corpus-wide mention intensity, work prevalence and between-scientist ordering remain unestimated. Sampled editions and access outcomes (CSV)

Name rules on identical pages. In the separately identified historical materials, compare the expanded biographical spelling, registered spellings and mentions adjudicated in context using the same denominators. For example, two pages of the Cole memorial contain five occurrences under the registered spelling rule and seven after resolving the short form. In the 2017 manifestation of Zuckerman's book, four become six after checking initialed citations. These differences demonstrate which mentions an alias-only rule loses.
