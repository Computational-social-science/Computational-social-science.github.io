The Matthew
Effect

Recognition and credit in science

For comparable scientific contributions, an established scientist can receive more credit than a lesser-known peer because of their prior standing. Who identified this pattern, who explained it, and who gave it a name?

19 May 1964 Earliest public print located in this investigation

Attribution in the scientific account

Public naming
The 19 May 1964 issue of The Statesman attributes the naming to Merton. The exact date of the lecture and absolute priority remain open. Read the contemporary report and its dating.
Research contributions
In her account, Zuckerman describes recognizing a recurring pattern in her interviews and credits Merton with naming and explaining it. Merton’s 1968 paper identifies her interviews as an empirical source. Read their accounts of the work.
Publication credit
Merton was the sole named author of the 1968 paper. He later wrote that both researchers merited the byline. Read the authorship acknowledgment.
